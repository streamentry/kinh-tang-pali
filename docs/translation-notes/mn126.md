## Notes

- From-scratch translation (181 segments). Structure: prince Jayasena asks Bhūmija whether the brahmacariya bears fruit "having made a wish / not / both / neither" (the fatalists' catuṣkoṭa). Bhūmija answers — admitting he speaks only by inference — that the decisive factor is rational vs irrational practice: whoever leads the life ayoniso cannot win the fruit, whoever leads it yoniso can, regardless of wishing. After the meal the Buddha confirms verbatim ("Thật vậy, này Bhūmija...") and unfolds the principle: those with the wrong eight factors cannot attain the fruit; those with the right eight can. Eight similes in two mirrored sets of four — irrational: pressing sand for oil, pulling a newly-calved cow's horn for milk, churning water for butter, drilling green sappy wood for fire; rational: pressing sesame flour for oil, pulling the udder, churning curds, drilling dried wood — each with the fourfold wish-variation, "Vì sao vậy?" and the ayoni/yoni verdict. Close: had these four similes occurred to Bhūmija, Jayasena would have gained confidence; "how could they occur to me... as to the Buddha?"
