## Notes

- From-scratch translation (190 segments, including range keys `mn119:11-14.*` and `mn119:15-17.*`). After the monks praise mindfulness of the body, the Buddha explains its cultivation fourteen ways, each closing with the refrain on giving up household memories-and-thoughts and the mind settling inwardly: breathing mindfulness, postures, clear comprehension, the thirty-two parts (bag-of-grains simile), the four elements (butcher simile), nine charnel-ground contemplations, and the four jhānas each pervading the body with its flavour (bath-powder, spring-fed lake, lotuses, white cloth similes). Mindfulness of the body includes all realization-related skillful states (ocean simile); undeveloped, it gives Māra a breach (heavy stone in wet clay, drill on dry wood, water into empty jar); developed, no breach (ball of string on heartwood door, drill on green wood, brimming jar); whatever is to be realized lies within range (tilted jar, walled pond, thoroughbred chariot). Ten benefits: mastering discontent and dread, enduring hardships and painful feelings, the four jhānas at will, psychic power, clairaudience, the sixteen mind-reading pairs (abbreviated per root), past lives, divine eye, and the ending of defilements with undeluded freedom of heart and wisdom.
