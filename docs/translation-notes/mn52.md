## High-risk audit points

- The discourse presents eleven `amatadvārāni`: four jhānas, four brahmavihāra mind-releases, and the first three immaterial attainments through the dimension of nothingness.
- The liberating move is not mere attainment: each attained state is known as `abhisaṅkhata/abhisañcetayita`, hence impermanent and subject to cessation.
- `dhammarāga/dhammanandi` is retained as attachment/delight toward that Dhamma or meditative state; if full destruction of the taints is not reached, the text gives the non-returner trajectory after destruction of the five lower fetters.
- Repetitions elided by `…pe…` in Pāli/English are not invented as new doctrine; the Vietnamese makes the repeated logical content explicit while staying within the supplied formula.
- `amatadvāra` is rendered “cánh cửa bất tử” in the householder's simile, without turning it into a claim that the meditative state itself is an eternal substance.
