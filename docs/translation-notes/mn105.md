## Notes

- From-scratch translation (188 segments). Sunakkhatta questions the monks' declarations of final knowledge; the Buddha distinguishes right declarations from ones made "do tăng thượng mạn", recounts being on the verge of teaching when "những kẻ ngu si" come with rehearsed questions, then delivers the discourse: the five strands of sense pleasure, and the five tiers of inclination ("hướng về") — worldly-fleshly things, the imperturbable, the dimension of nothingness, the dimension of neither-perception-nor-non-perception, and right intent on extinguishment — each shown by its own simile (long-absent villager's news, withered leaf, split boulder, vomited delicacy, crown-cut palm) as released from the fetter of the tier below. Then the craving-dart / ignorance-poison core: a monk's unfounded "mũi tên ái của ta đã được từ bỏ" plays out through both arrow-surgery similes (with/without residual poison, including the surgeon's full aftercare instructions), death being defined as "từ bỏ học pháp và trở lại đời sống thấp hạ" and deadly pain as "tội cấu uế" (22.1–22.2); 27.3–27.8 maps the simile (wound = six internal sense fields, poison = ignorance, dart = craving, probe = mindfulness, scalpel = noble wisdom, surgeon = the Tathāgata). Closes with the "Thủ là gốc của khổ" refrain and the poisoned cup and viper similes.
