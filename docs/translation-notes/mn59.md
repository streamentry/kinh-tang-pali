## High-risk audit points

- Udāyī and Pañcakaṅga are both treated as correct because the Buddha uses multiple valid classifications of feeling: 2, 3, 5, 6, 18, 36, and 108.
- Neutral feeling remains `adukkhamasukhā vedanā`; in Pañcakaṅga's twofold scheme it is counted with peaceful, refined happiness rather than deleted.
- The hierarchy of happiness runs from sensual pleasure through the four jhānas and four immaterial attainments to cessation of perception and feeling.
- The final clarification is decisive: the Buddha's use of `sukha` is broader than pleasant feeling (`sukhā vedanā`) alone. Cessation can therefore be designated as happiness without positing a pleasant feeling there.
- The disagreement scene preserves the practical warning that multiple valid analytical schemes can generate conflict when speakers refuse to recognize each other's well-spoken formulation.
