## High-risk audit points

- The deliberate terminology contrast `daṇḍa` vs `kamma` is preserved as `phạt` vs `nghiệp`; body, speech, and mind remain separately aligned.
- `cetanā` is translated as intention/volition, not consciousness, because its classification under the mental rod is a load-bearing step in the debate.
- The cold-water/hot-water example contains an apparent internal tension in the Pāli; the translation preserves rather than silently repairs it.
- Supernormal examples about destroying Nāḷandā and the ancient wildernesses are translated as claims inside the discourse, not naturalized or allegorized.
- Upāli's conversion is not presented as blind faith: the Buddha explicitly tells a prominent person to act after careful consideration.
- The Buddha's instruction to keep giving food to Jain ascetics is preserved alongside Upāli's later restriction on their entering his house.
- The gradual teaching and arising of the Dhamma eye are kept in sequence and without added doctrinal gloss.
- The monkey/dyeing simile is kept as an argument about withstanding scrutiny, not merely attractiveness.
- The long praise poem is aligned line-by-line with Pāli. Rare epithets are rendered conservatively and explicitly flagged rather than over-interpreted.
- The final account of Nāṭaputta coughing/vomiting hot blood is retained as the text's claim without medical speculation.
