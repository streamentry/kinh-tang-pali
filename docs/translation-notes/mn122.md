## Notes

- From-scratch translation (172 segments). Structure: at Kapilavatthu's Banyan monastery the Buddha sees many beds spread at Khemaka-the-Dark's lodge, asks Ānanda (robe-making season), then teaches: one who delights in company and groups shines not and cannot gain the pleasures of renunciation/seclusion/peace/awakening nor the temporary-pleasant or irreversible-unshakable freedom of heart; no sight is safe from sorrow at its change for one attached to it. The Tathāgata's discovered dwelling: entering internal emptiness by not attending to any sign; when visitors come his mind bent on seclusion gives only "sending-off" talk. Method: still/settle/unify/immerse the mind via the four jhānas (…pe… elided), then attend to internal → external → internal-external emptiness → the imperturbable, with honest awareness when the mind does or does not "hướng đến, tịnh tín, an trú, giải thoát"; postures (walking, standing, sitting, lying) free of covetousness-and-displeasure; abandoning the 27 kinds of animal talk and cultivating the 10 noble talks (self-effacement…); thought-training renouncing sensual/malicious/cruel thoughts for renunciation/good-will/harmlessness; regularly checking the mind's stirrings toward the five sense-pleasures (chandarāga test); contemplating rise-and-fall in the five grasping aggregates until the conceit "I am" is abandoned. Closing: these principles are wholly skillful, transcendent, inaccessible to Māra; a disciple follows the teacher not for texts but for the self-effacing talk; the perils of teacher, pupil and celibate practitioner (seclusion undone by visitors); "treat me as a friend, not an enemy"; the potter simile — "censuring again and again, pressing again and again, I will speak; the core will stand firm."
