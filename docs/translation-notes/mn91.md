## Notes

- From-scratch translation (308 segments). The 120-year-old Brahmin Brahmāyu sends his student Uttara to verify the Buddha's reputation via the thirty-two marks of a great man; Uttara shadows the Buddha for seven months and reports his deportment in exhaustive detail (walking, sitting, eating, teaching — with the eight qualities of voice); Brahmāyu then visits, has his two doubts (sheathed privates, long tongue) resolved by psychic display, asks in verse how one becomes brahmin/knowledge-master/three-knowledge-master/scholar/adept/consummate/sage/Buddha, receives the answer in verse, prostrates ("con là bà-la-môn Brahmāyu!"), converts, feeds the Sangha for seven days, dies soon after as a non-returner.
- The thirty-two marks and the deportment catalogue are translated mark-for-mark per root segments; the two doubted marks mirror each other between Uttara's and Brahmāyu's inspections.
- TMC consulted for the marks phrasing; verses rendered to match the established repo idiom.
