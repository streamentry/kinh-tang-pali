## High-risk audit points

- The four water-pot similes and royal war-elephant simile preserve the force of `sampajānamusāvāda`: knowingly/deliberately lying, even in jest.
- The mirror simile is carried through as repeated examination of bodily, verbal, and mental action before, during, and after the act.
- Harm to self, harm to others, and harm to both are not collapsed into a generic “bad outcome”.
- After a bad bodily or verbal act, confession/disclosure to the Teacher or a wise fellow practitioner is preserved; after a bad mental act, the distinct Pāli formula of distress/shame/disgust is preserved.
- `sasakkaṁ` is rendered conservatively as doing one’s utmost to refrain.
- `mn61:12.8` follows the pinned Pāli root exactly even though the parallel formula and comparison translations contain an additional “harm to both” clause.
- Ancient elephant vocabulary (`īsādanto`, `urūḷhavā`) is rendered conservatively and documented rather than given a falsely exact modern technical equivalent.
