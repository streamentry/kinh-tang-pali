## Notes

- From-scratch translation (232 segments). Structure: at the Hot Springs Monastery Samiddhi, drying off after a night bath, is quizzed by a radiant deity — neither knows the one-fine-night summary-and-analysis nor even the verses; "learn it, it is beneficial, ādibrahmacariyaka". Samiddhi reports; the Buddha recites the sixteen verses (reused verbatim from mn131:3.x) and enters his dwelling. The monks deliberate (verse quoted, then twice abbreviated) and go to Mahākaccāna, who first rebukes them with the heartwood simile (bypassing root and trunk — the Buddha himself — to seek heartwood in the branches), then, pressed again, analyzes via the six sense-bases: for each of eye-and-forms etc., past ("ta đã có mắt này... đã có các sắc này" — consciousness bound by desire-and-lust → delighting → running back), future ("mong rằng ta sẽ có..." — the heart set on gaining the un-gained → anticipating), present (both presently arisen — bound → faltering), each with its negative counterpart. The monks report back (verse full again inside nested quotes); the Buddha praises Mahākaccāna ("bậc hiền trí, bậc đại tuệ — I would answer the same").
