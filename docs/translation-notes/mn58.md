## High-risk audit points

- The Nigaṇṭha dilemma is preserved as a deliberately two-sided trap; the Buddha does not accept its yes/no framing.
- The six speech cases preserve all three decision dimensions: truth/falsity, benefit/non-benefit, and whether the speech is pleasing or displeasing.
- Truth alone is not sufficient for speech. When speech is both true and beneficial, timing still matters.
- The child-with-an-object-in-the-mouth simile preserves compassion as the explicit reason for an intervention that may cause bleeding.
- `dhammadhātu` is rendered contextually as `nguyên lý của Pháp`, with the traditional `pháp giới` reading documented rather than silently turned into a metaphysical claim.
- The spontaneous-answer passage is not expanded into omniscience about every proposition; it is grounded in penetration of the relevant Dhamma principle.
- Devadatta's hell/eon statement and other supernatural claims are translated as claims in the text, not naturalized or allegorized.
